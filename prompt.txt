# Project: Professional Inventory & POS Management System

Act as a senior full-stack software engineer and UI/UX designer. Build a complete, professional, fast, and reliable Inventory Management System using React and Firebase.

The system should be simple to operate, professionally designed, responsive, and suitable for daily business operations.

Do not overcomplicate the application. Use clean, reusable components, optimized code, and a well-structured architecture.

## 6. Sales / POS Module

This is one of the most important modules.

Create a fast and professional Point of Sale interface.

Layout:
- Product search section.
- Barcode scanning input.
- Product selection area.
- Shopping cart.
- Customer name field.
- Sale summary section.

Sales workflow:

1. Scan a product barcode using a USB barcode scanner.
2. Automatically find the matching product.
3. Add the product to the cart.
4. If the same product is scanned again, increase its quantity.
5. Allow manual product searching and selection.
6. Allow quantity adjustments.
7. Allow removing products from the cart.
8. Automatically calculate subtotal.
9. Allow discount entry.
10. Calculate final payable amount.
11. Enter customer name.
12. Generate and save the sale.
13. Automatically deduct sold quantities from inventory.
14. Generate the invoice.
15. Provide direct invoice printing.

Sale information:

- Unique Invoice Number.
- Customer Name.
- Sale Date and Time.
- Product Name.
- Product Barcode.
- Quantity.
- Unit Price.
- Discount.
- Subtotal.
- Grand Total.
- Payment Amount.
- Remaining Amount (optional).
- Payment Status.

For the initial version, keep the payment process simple. Support cash sales and optional paid/remaining amounts.

IMPORTANT:
Use Firestore transactions or atomic write operations to ensure that stock deduction and sale creation remain consistent.

Prevent duplicate submissions and overselling when multiple requests occur simultaneously.

## 16. Firestore Database Structure

Use a clean and scalable Firestore structure:

users/
  {userId}

categories/
  {categoryId}

products/
  {productId}

sales/
  {saleId}

salesReturns/
  {returnId}

stockMovements/
  {movementId}

settings/
  {settingsId}

Each document must have appropriate IDs, timestamps, and relationships.

Use Firestore indexes for common queries.

Store product IDs inside sales rather than relying only on product names.

For financial records, preserve historical product prices at the time of sale so that editing the current product price does not change old invoices.

Use server timestamps where appropriate.
